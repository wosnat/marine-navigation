World Ocean Atlas 2023: an overview

The World Ocean Atlas (WOA) is NOAA's gridded, long-term average picture of the ocean's interior: temperature, salinity, oxygen and nutrients, from the surface down to 5,500 m. These pages describe what it contains, how to use it in this project, and what it looks like.

What it is

WOA23 is produced by NOAA's National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI), Ocean Climate Laboratory, and was released in February 2024. It is built from the World Ocean Database, the largest public collection of ocean profile measurements: ship casts (CTD and bottles), expendable probes, gliders and, since the mid-2000s, mostly Argo floats. WOA23 added about 3.1 million casts compared with WOA18.

The scattered measurements are quality-controlled, averaged into grid boxes at fixed ("standard") depths, then objectively analyzed. That is, they are smoothed and interpolated so that every ocean grid cell has a value, even where nobody ever measured. The result is a climatology: the typical state of the ocean for a given month, season or year, averaged over many years. It is not a snapshot of any particular day.

Why it matters for marine navigation

Many migrating animals (turtles, eels, salmon, tunas, whales) cross ocean basins and come back to specific places. Besides the geomagnetic field, candidate cues include gradients in water properties that an animal can sense directly: temperature, salinity and, indirectly, chemistry. WOA gives us a consistent global map of these properties in three dimensions, so we can ask:

  • Where are the gradients (fronts) strong enough to be detectable, and which direction do they point?
  • How stable are these patterns across seasons and decades, and therefore how reliable are they as cues?
  • How do the patterns change with depth, for animals that dive or migrate at depth?
Limits to keep in mind. WOA is a smoothed long-term average. It hides eddies, day-to-day variability and fine-scale fronts that an animal actually experiences. It also has no currents, magnetic field or bathymetry; those come from other datasets.
