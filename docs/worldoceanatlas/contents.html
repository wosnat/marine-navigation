What's in the World Ocean Atlas

WOA23 is organized along five axes: variable × time span (which years) × time period (annual, season or month) × grid (resolution) × statistical field (mean, count, error…). Each netCDF file holds one variable, one time span, one period and one grid, with all statistical fields and all depths inside.

Grids

Grid cells are centered on half degrees: on the 1° grid, latitudes run −89.5 … 89.5 and longitudes −179.5 … 179.5.

Number of cells (1° grid)

The globe has 360 × 180 = 64,800 cells per depth level. Of these, 41,088 are ocean at the surface, 36,243 at 1,000 m and 22,193 at 4,000 m (the seafloor rises above the deeper levels in more places). Across all 102 levels there are about 3.3 million ocean cells. The ¼° grid has 16× as many cells (1,440 × 720 = 1,036,800 per level).

How big is a cell?

One degree of latitude is always about 111 km. One degree of longitude shrinks toward the poles (111 km × cos(latitude)):

Latitude1° cell (east–west × north–south)Area
Equator111 × 111 km~12,300 km²
30° (Florida, Canary Islands)96 × 111 km~10,700 km²
45° (Bay of Biscay)79 × 111 km~8,700 km²
60° (Shetland, Gulf of Alaska)56 × 111 km~6,200 km²
Because cells shrink toward the poles, global averages must be area-weighted by cos(latitude). Also, the smoothing in the objective analysis spreads each measurement over hundreds of kilometres, so features smaller than that are blurred even on the 1° grid.

Data sources and coverage

WOA uses no satellite data. It is built only from in-situ measurements in the World Ocean Database: ship casts (CTD and water bottles), expendable temperature probes dropped from ships, Argo floats, gliders, moorings and drifting buoys. Satellites only see the surface, so they could not help with the interior anyway.

Every ocean cell has an _an value, but many cells have no measurement at all and are filled in from their neighbours. The _dd field counts the observations in each cell.

What this means

  • Temperature and salinity are well sampled down to about 1,000–2,000 m, mostly thanks to Argo floats, which profile between 2,000 m and the surface. Below 2,000 m only ships reach, and half of the deep ocean has no temperature measurement at all.
  • Older decades are thin at depth. Before Argo, only about a quarter of the ocean was ever measured at 1,000 m.
  • Oxygen is moderate: about three-quarters of the upper ocean has data, but only a handful of measurements per cell.
  • Nutrients are sparse: only about a third of the ocean has any measurement, almost all from research ships. Those maps are mostly interpolation, so fine-scale patterns in them should not be trusted.
  • "Measured" does not mean the map shows the measurement. _an is smoothed everywhere. The raw cell average is in _mn, and _oa shows how far the smoothing moved it.

The time axis is coarse as well: WOA holds only multi-year averages (a typical month, season or year over a span of years), never the value for a particular month of a particular year.

Standard depth levels

102 levels from 0 to 5,500 m, spaced more finely near the surface, where the ocean changes fastest:

Depth rangeSpacingLevels
0 – 100 m5 m1 – 21
100 – 500 m25 m21 – 37
500 – 2,000 m50 m37 – 67
2,000 – 5,500 m100 m67 – 102

Monthly files stop at 1,500 m (level 57); seasonal and monthly nutrient files stop at 800 m (level 43). Below the seafloor, values are missing (NaN).

File naming

woa23_[span]_[v][period]_[grid].nc

woa23_decav_t00_01.nc    temperature, 1955–2022 average, annual,      1°
woa23_decav_s15_04.nc    salinity,    1955–2022 average, Jul–Sep,     ¼°
woa23_all_o07_01.nc      oxygen,      all years,         July,        1°
woa23_B5C2_t00_01.nc     temperature, 2015–2022,         annual,      1°

Files live at https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/oceans/woa/WOA23/DATA/<variable>/netcdf/<span>/<1.00|0.25|5.00>/. A 1° annual temperature file is about 85 MB. Besides netCDF, the data are also offered as CSV, shapefiles and a legacy ASCII format, but netCDF is the easiest to work with.

Masks

WOA23/MASKS/ holds landsea_01.msk (the standard level at which the seafloor is reached in each cell) and basinmask_01.msk (60 numbered basins, such as Atlantic Ocean, Mediterranean Sea or North American Basin, which can change with depth), both also available at ¼°.

Source: Reagan et al. (2024), WOA23 Product Documentation, NOAA NCEI.
