What's in the World Ocean Atlas

WOA23 is organized along five axes: variable × time span (which years) × time period (annual, season or month) × grid (resolution) × statistical field (mean, count, error…). Each netCDF file holds one variable, one time span, one period and one grid, with all statistical fields and all depths inside.

The derived quantities that WOA18 also offered (density, conductivity, mixed layer depth) are not in the WOA23 data folder. If we need them we can compute them from T and S with the GSW (TEOS-10) package.

Temperature and salinity have no all span, and oxygen and nutrients have no decadal spans. When comparing T or S with oxygen or nutrients, we compare decav with all, which cover nearly the same years.

Grid cells are centered on half degrees: on the 1° grid, latitudes run −89.5 … 89.5 and longitudes −179.5 … 179.5.

Standard depth levels

102 levels from 0 to 5,500 m, spaced more finely near the surface, where the ocean changes fastest:

Depth rangeSpacingLevels
0 – 100 m5 m1 – 21
100 – 500 m25 m21 – 37
500 – 2,000 m50 m37 – 67
2,000 – 5,500 m100 m67 – 102

Monthly files stop at 1,500 m (level 57); seasonal and monthly nutrient files stop at 800 m (level 43). Below the seafloor, values are missing (NaN).

File naming

woa23_[span]_[v][period]_[grid].nc

woa23_decav_t00_01.nc    temperature, 1955–2022 average, annual,      1°
woa23_decav_s15_04.nc    salinity,    1955–2022 average, Jul–Sep,     ¼°
woa23_all_o07_01.nc      oxygen,      all years,         July,        1°
woa23_B5C2_t00_01.nc     temperature, 2015–2022,         annual,      1°

Files live at https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/oceans/woa/WOA23/DATA/<variable>/netcdf/<span>/<1.00|0.25|5.00>/. A 1° annual temperature file is about 85 MB. Besides netCDF, the data are also offered as CSV, shapefiles and a legacy ASCII format, but netCDF is the easiest to work with.

Masks

WOA23/MASKS/ holds landsea_01.msk (the standard level at which the seafloor is reached in each cell) and basinmask_01.msk (60 numbered basins, such as Atlantic Ocean, Mediterranean Sea or North American Basin, which can change with depth), both also available at ¼°.

Source: Reagan et al. (2024), WOA23 Product Documentation, NOAA NCEI.
